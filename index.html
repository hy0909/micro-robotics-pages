Finding a mosquito is only the first step.

Micro Robotics develops AI and precision control technology for detecting and tracking mosquitoes. To act on a detection, a system needs to keep following the mosquito as it moves.

We are developing a fixed research platform to test the process, from recognising a mosquito in camera footage to tracking it and controlling the laser.

Current research

From finding a mosquito to responding to it.

Our approach brings detection, tracking and laser control into one system. Alongside the response itself, we want to record where and when mosquitoes appear, so the data can help inform mosquito control.

ApproachOur researchRole and considerations
Chemical control & manual work—Uses insecticides and on-site work to reduce mosquito numbers. Recording where and how often mosquitoes appear adds information for planning and reviewing that work.
Ultrasonic detection—Uses sound for detection. Indoor reflections and detection range are factors to assess.
Location marking—Shows where a mosquito was detected. Following its movement and responding require additional functions.

The technology behind
each step.

Our work brings together mosquito recognition, motion tracking and laser control. The next challenge is making the hardware small and light enough for a flying robot.

01

Recognising mosquitoes

We use near-infrared and colour cameras to identify mosquitoes, including work on detection in low light.

02

Following their movement

We analyse changes in position to estimate the mosquito’s path and time the system’s response.

03

Controlling the laser

We are studying laser-based mosquito control with a separate safety controller that limits when the laser can operate.

04

Building for flight

We plan to reduce the size and weight of the sensing and control modules so they can be carried by a small flying robot.

From a fixed platform to a flying robot.

Once the sensing and control functions have been tested on the fixed platform, we plan to make the modules smaller and lighter. Our long-term goal is a small robot that can perceive its surroundings and fly autonomously in confined spaces.
